### Candidate 1: Metrogas (METR:BYMA), new, watchlist

**The business.** Licensed natural gas distributor for Buenos Aires city and the southern conurbation, licence from 1992. Class B shares trade on BYMA; no ADR. There is no US listing, so this line would not be tradeable in the real IBKR account (A1 item 2 requires this to be stated).

**Event.** Three dated things, all documented. (1) On 10 Aug 2026 YPF accepted Edenor's offer of US$780m for its entire stake: 290,277,316 Class A and 108,142,529 Class B shares of Metrogas (70% of capital and votes) plus 11,500 shares (5%) of MetroEnergía (Metrogas hecho relevante, 11 Aug 2026). (2) On 29 Sep 2026 Metrogas signed a 20-year licence extension with the Ministry of Economy, running from 28 Dec 2027 to Dec 2047, with a US$186,775,505.56 investment commitment and withdrawal of tariff claims against the state; the extraordinary shareholders' meeting of 29 Oct approved it unanimously with 88.33% of capital present; documents go to the Executive by 13 Nov 2026 and the Executive then has 60 business days to ratify (iProfesional, 29 Oct). (3) Economía suspended ENARGAS's divestment order against YPF by Resolution 1469/2026 (Boletín Oficial, 8 Sep). Edenor is expected to take control in December once approvals complete (iProfesional, 29 Oct).

**Why the price may be wrong.** A change of control in an Argentine listed company obliges the acquirer to launch a mandatory tender offer for the remaining shares (La Nación, 11 Sep; MercoPress, 12 Aug, both naming ANSES, the float and Manzano's 9.23% as the holders covered). Arithmetic: US$780m over 398,419,845 Metrogas shares is US$1.958 per share, treating the 5% MetroEnergía stake as worth nothing, which overstates the per-share number slightly. 398,419,845 is 70%, so there are about 569.2m shares in total. At the wholesale rate of ARS 1,551 per dollar on 3 Nov (Infobae, 3 Nov) that is ARS 3,036 per share against a close of ARS 2,340 on 3 Nov (Rava), a gap of about 30%. The market is either discounting the OPA price rule, the timing, or the peso. The minority holders are ANSES (a mandated holder) and a retail float; the seller into a 30% spread with a dated process is not obviously informed.

**Path and date.** Executive ratification of the licence (deadline about mid-February 2027 if the 60 business days run from 13 Nov), ENRGE approval of Edenor as controller, closing (guided December), then the mandatory offer, which under CNV rules must be launched within a short window after control passes and takes a further one to two months to settle [UNVERIFIED: the CNV Normas Título III Cap. II timing and the price rule were not read this week]. Working catalyst date: **2027-03-31** for the OPA to be launched, with settlement by mid-2027. Slip scenario: closing slips a quarter and the offer slips with it; the position then holds a regulated gas distributor with a 20-year licence at a price below the control price, which is tolerable, as long as the peso does not do what the real did on 27 Oct.

**What would have to be true.** (1) The Argentine mandatory-offer price rule sets the floor at the price paid for control, in dollars or in pesos indexed, so the ARS 3,036 is a floor and not a ceiling that devalues with the peso. (2) Edenor closes: it has to fund US$780m, and Edenor's own balance sheet and leverage after the purchase are [UNVERIFIED]. (3) The ANSES stake does not get a side arrangement that lets the offeror avoid a full offer.

**Bear case, longer.** The offer price is the whole trade and this memo has not read the rule that sets it; if CNV lets the offeror use a six-month average or a valuation report instead of the control price, the 30% gap is a fiction. The price will be stated in pesos at some date; between now and settlement the official rate has been making nominal records (Infobae, 3 Nov), and the CCL rate a foreign holder actually gets is wider than the wholesale 1,551 by a gap the same article calls elevated, so the dollar value of the offer shrinks with every month of delay. Argentine mandatory offers have historically been slow and litigated; a delay of two quarters is the base case, not the tail. Edenor's bid was the highest of five and 39% above the process valuation (Shale24, Aug 2026): a buyer that overpaid has an incentive to make the minority offer as cheap and as late as the rules allow, and the same group (Manzano) already sits on 9.23% of Metrogas, so it is on both sides. Edenor is itself an electricity distributor with a history of tariff-driven cash squeezes; if it finances the purchase with debt the acquirer could become the problem. The licence extension carries a US$186.8m investment commitment and the withdrawal of tariff claims; the tariff-review history (2017 integral review, Resolution 508/2017) is litigation the company is giving up. Liquidity: ARS 409m traded on 3 Nov, about US$264k at 1,551 (Rava, Infobae), which clears the US$150k floor on one day's evidence only; the 15%-of-ADV rule caps a position at about US$40k on that day, so a US$4k paper line is fine, but a real line would be thin. Finally the gates the memo could not clear this week: positive operating cash flow in two of the last three years, leverage, auditor, and whether the regulated tariff path under the new licence is readable in English or Spanish filings only (the mandate wants English or Portuguese; Spanish primary documents are a stretch of the rule the user should rule on).

**Numbers.** US$780m for 398,419,845 Metrogas shares plus 11,500 MetroEnergía shares (Metrogas HR, 11 Aug 2026). Licence to Dec 2047, investment US$186,775,505.56, AGE 29 Oct 2026 with 88.33% present, Executive deadline 60 business days from a 13 Nov filing (iProfesional, 29 Oct 2026). Resolution 1469/2026, Boletín Oficial 8 Sep 2026. Close ARS 2,340, volume 174,660 shares, ARS 409,241,706 (Rava, 3 Nov 2026). Wholesale dollar ARS 1,551 (Infobae, 3 Nov 2026).
