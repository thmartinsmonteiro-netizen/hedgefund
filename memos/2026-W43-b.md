### Candidate 1: Brown Advisory US Smaller Companies (BASC:LSE), the one with a real closer
**The business.** A London-listed investment trust holding US small-cap equities, managed by Brown Advisory, net assets about £186m (total assets £185.555m at 1 Oct 2026, RNS of 2 Oct 2026). Five-year total investment return 14% against 46% for the Russell 2000 (QuotedData, 7 Oct 2026; AIC, 8 Jul 2026).

**The event.** On 7 Oct 2026 the board announced a scheme of reconstruction: BASC winds up and shareholders either roll into JPMorgan US Smaller Companies (JUSC) or take an **uncapped cash exit at Residual NAV less 0.75%**, where Residual NAV deducts BASC's transaction costs not already in NAV and a liquidators' retention of at most £100,000. Circulars are expected by mid-November 2026, completion in December 2026 "or shortly after". Saba Capital, about 16% of BASC, has irrevocably undertaken to vote for the scheme and take cash, and has a standstill with JUSC to 7 Oct 2027. Conditions: JUSC shareholders approve the share issue, BASC shareholders approve the scheme and the members' voluntary liquidation, plus "certain regulatory and tax approvals"; the Takeover Code is not expected to apply (JUSC RNS, 7 Oct 2026, via Investegate).

**Why the price might be wrong.** Before the announcement both trusts traded about 9% below NAV; BASC rose 2.2% to £16.45 on the day (QuotedData, 7 Oct 2026). Who would be the uninformed seller now? Nobody obvious: a £180m trust with a 16% activist, retail holders who are being offered a tax-free rollover, and an uncapped cash exit. The residual discount is not a mispricing; it is the market's price for 10 weeks of US small-cap exposure, completion risk and the exit haircut. That is the finding, and it is why there is no trade.

**Numbers, each with source and date.**
- Price: 1,640p, close 21 Oct 2026 (Yahoo quote page, marked "at close", retrieved today; also the manual row in data/prices.csv). Previous close 1,610p. Market cap £179.9m. Average volume 12,050 shares (Yahoo), about £198k or US$264k a day at GBPUSD 1.3375 (data/prices.csv, 21 Oct). Passes the US$150k floor; a 5%-of-NAV paper position (about 230 shares) would be 1.9% of ADV, inside the fast-sleeve rule of 5%.
- NAV: 1,702.25p actual at 15 Oct 2026 and 1,683.33p estimated at 16 Oct (AJ Bell quote page, retrieved today); 1,691.62p cum-income at 1 Oct 2026 (RNS, 2 Oct 2026). Discount at 1,640p: 3.7% to the 15 Oct actual, 2.6% to the 16 Oct estimate. The NAV on 21 Oct is not published yet, so the spread below carries one week of Russell 2000 drift [UNVERIFIED].
- Shares in issue: about 10.97m, derived from £185.555m / 1,691.62p; not a reported figure.
- Bid/offer on 16 Oct: 1,620p / 1,645p (AJ Bell), a 1.5% touch. On a real order the entry cost is the half-spread, not the fund's 0.25% assumption.

**The arithmetic that says no.** Exit value per share = 1,702.25p × 0.9925 = 1,689.5p before Residual NAV deductions; assume 0.4% of NAV for transaction costs and the retention [assumption, not disclosed] gives about 1,683p. Against 1,640p that is a gross 2.6% over about 10 weeks. Hedged with a short in a Russell 2000 ETF (allowed under amendment A1, against a dated event), the fund's cost model charges 0.4% on each side of the long leg and 0.35% on each side of the short leg, about 1.5% of the long leg in total; the short proceeds earn the 4% cash rate and borrow is assumed at 1%. On a US$5,000 pair the expected result is about US$85 if the scheme completes on time and the hedge is perfect, against US$39 from leaving the money in cash, so about US$45 of excess before any break probability. A 5% chance that the scheme fails and the discount reopens to 9% costs about US$17 of that. Net expected value: roughly US$30, 0.03% of NAV, on a position that occupies a fast-sleeve slot and real attention. Unhedged, the trade is a Russell 2000 bet with a 2.6% cushion, which is not the thesis.

**What would make it a trade.** Discount ≥ 5% to the last published NAV while the scheme stays on timetable (net spread then ≥ 2.5% on the same cost model, about US$125 of expected value per US$5,000 pair). That can happen on a small-cap sell-off, when the trust's retail holders sell into a thin book, or if the circular slips. The entry rule is logged in pipeline.csv so the next memo has no discretion about it.

**Bear case, which is also the case for the watchlist rather than the bin.** The scheme needs 75% of votes cast at a BASC general meeting; Saba's 16% helps but a low-turnout retail register can fail special resolutions, and the 2025 Saba campaigns showed turnout is the whole game. The "regulatory and tax approvals" are not named in the RNS; a UK scheme of this type usually needs HMRC clearance for the rollover, which can slip into January. If it slips, the hedge costs and borrow run and the spread does not grow. The hedge is imperfect: BASC holds quality-growth small caps, not the Russell 2000; in a month where small-cap value rallies and growth lags, a 5% pair can lose 2 to 3% on basis alone, which is larger than the spread. Residual NAV deductions are not disclosed; the 0.4% assumption could be 0.8%. The other side of the trade is Saba, who negotiated the terms and is taking cash: it is not selling to us, it is exiting with us, which is comforting on completion risk and tells us nothing about the spread being wrong. Finally, the fund's own trap 1: a scheme with an uncapped NAV exit is the most studied, most crowded pocket of the UK trust market after two years of Saba; a 3% discount ten weeks from cash is what efficient looks like.

**Verdict.** Watchlist, paper, with the trigger above. Not a position. Disclosure per A1: no professional knowledge of BASC, JUSC, Brown Advisory or JPMorgan on my side; yours to state.

### Candidate 2: Kyocera (6971:TSE), the one with a mechanism and no discount
**The business.** Japanese ceramics and electronic components group with a strategic stake in KDDI. FY2026 (to March 2027) guidance: sales JPY 2,020bn, operating profit JPY 100bn (strategy release, 3 Feb 2026, via FilingReader).

**The mechanism.** Announced 3 Feb 2026: sell JPY 250bn of KDDI shares in FY2026 and another JPY 250bn in FY2027; buy back JPY 200bn of own shares in FY2026 and up to JPY 500bn more through 2028; dividend on equity from FY2027; ROE targets 5% by FY2028 and 8% by FY2031 (same release). First leg executed: 53,681,800 KDDI shares sold into KDDI's own tender at JPY 2,325, proceeds about JPY 124.8bn, stake down from 14.77% to 13.36% (TipRanks summary of the company notice, updated 11 Jun 2026). Activist: Oasis launched a second campaign on 15 May 2025 asking for a JPY 1tn buyback over four years and divestments, citing ROE of 0.8% and cross-shareholdings above JPY 1.7tn, 53% of net assets (Oasis release via AI Journal, 15 May 2025). That is three of the screen's named closers at once: announced disposal of the stake, buyback, activist with a record.

**Why it fails: the discount is not there.** KDDI market cap JPY 11.65tn at JPY 3,060 (StockAnalysis, 18 Sep 2026); 13.36% of that is about JPY 1.56tn. Kyocera market cap JPY 4.686tn at JPY 3,668 (Yahoo quote page, retrieved today; the page's performance data is dated 2 Oct 2026, so treat the price as approximate), P/B 1.39 (Yahoo, as of 18 Sep 2026). Market cap less the KDDI stake leaves about JPY 3.13tn for everything else, which is about 31x this year's forecast operating profit of JPY 100bn, before counting the other cross-holdings, which would push the core multiple higher still. A holdco trade needs the parts to be worth more than the whole; here the whole is worth more than the parts unless you believe the core business is worth 30x operating profit. The mechanism is real and the market has already paid for it: the 52-week range is JPY 1,937 to 4,019 (Yahoo), so the stock roughly doubled into the strategy change.

**Bear case against my rejection, briefly.** A JPY 700bn buyback programme is about 15% of the market cap; if Oasis extracts its JPY 1tn the arithmetic changes, and the core businesses (fine ceramics, semiconductor packages) may deserve a high multiple in an AI-capex cycle. None of that is a lens-3 argument; it is a growth argument, and it belongs to another fund.

**Verdict.** Rejected, logged with the arithmetic in rejected.csv. Reconsider if the core implied value falls below 15x operating profit or a tender at a stated discount is announced.
