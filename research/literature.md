# Literature: what the evidence says, by lens

Living document. Every entry has a paper, a year, a link, the finding in one line, and what it changes in screens.md. Findings that change no rule do not go in. Entries marked [UNVERIFIED] are recalled from memory and must be replaced by a checked citation or deleted at the next research pass. Last pass: 2026-11-04 (supplement to the 2026-11-01 full pass: adds rows found in a second sweep, corrects the rights-issue entry reading, and proposes P6 to P9; the 2026-11-01 pass replaced all seed [UNVERIFIED] rows).

## How the fund uses this file
- The weekly memo cites the entry each candidate leans on, by lens and paper, or writes "no base rate available".
- A finding becomes a rule only through a dated amendment in mandate/amendments.md; this file is evidence, not policy. Candidate rule changes wait under "Proposed amendments" at the bottom until the user adopts or rejects them.
- Base rates from published samples are US-heavy and pre-2020 unless stated; the memo says when the situation is outside the sample (Brazil, Greece, small caps, post-2022 rates).
- Every number in this file appears in the cited paper's own text (abstract, introduction or tables as opened at the pass date). Practitioner sources (indices, vendor reports, trade bodies, blogs) are labelled as such and are pointers, not base rates.

## Lens 0: spin-offs

|---|---|---|
| Veld & Veld-Merkoulova, "Value creation through spin-offs: a review of the empirical evidence" (meta-analysis of 26 event studies), and their 2004 JBF European study, https://storre.stir.ac.uk/bitstream/1893/1813/1/value-creation-through-spin-offs-a-review-final.pdf | Announcement CAR about +3%; higher for larger, focus-increasing and tax-friendly spin-offs; long-run outperformance found in early US studies disappears with better tests, and European spin-offs show no long-run excess return | The announcement pop is not ours to capture. ## Lens 1: post-reorganisation equities
| Paper | Finding | Rule implication |
|---|---|---|

## Lens 2: merger arbitrage and tenders
| Paper | Finding | Rule implication |
|---|---|---|
| Buehlmaier & Zechner, "Financial media, price discovery, and merger arbitrage", Review of Finance 25(4), 2021, https://revfin.org/?p=2023 | Prices underreact to media-implied completion probability; dropping the 28% of deals with the lowest media-implied completion probability raised annualised alpha by 9.3 points; effect strongest when high-yield spreads are wide | Add a "completion-probability screen" to the merger memo: the memo must state a break probability with a source other than the spread itself, and skip deals where credible reporting flags regulatory or financing doubt. Wide-credit-spread periods are when the lens is worth the most |
| AllianceBernstein, "Merger arbitrage: riding the wave into 2026", Dec 2025 (practitioner, not a paper), https://www.alliancebernstein.com/us/en-us/investments/insights/investment-insights/merger-arbitrage-riding-the-wave-into-2026.html | HFRI merger-arb index +8.2% through Sep 2025; returns came from spread compression as regulatory risk fell | Context only: a benign regulatory regime compresses spreads, so the residual wide spreads are the ones with real break risk (trap 4) |
| Mitchell & Pulvino, "Characteristics of Risk and Return in Risk Arbitrage", Journal of Finance 56(6), 2001, https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v56y2001i6p2135-2175.html ; Baker & Savasoglu, "Limited arbitrage in mergers and acquisitions", Journal of Financial Economics 64(1), 2002, https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/357797 (abstract mirror; journal record https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v64y2002i1p91-115.html) | Mitchell & Pulvino, 4,750 mergers 1963–1998: returns are uncorrelated with the market in flat and rising markets and positively correlated in severely falling ones, i.e. the payoff of a written index put; after the non-linearity and transaction costs, excess returns of about four percent a year. Baker & Savasoglu, 1981–1996: a diversified portfolio earns an abnormal 0.6 to 0.9% a month, and returns rise with an ex-ante measure of completion risk and with target size, consistent with limited arbitrage capital | The category return is a few points a year with a crash beta; it does not pay for concentrated positions. Sizing is for the break, and the memo states what the position does in a severe market drawdown, when breaks cluster. The Baker–Savasoglu result says the premium sits in the deals with visible completion risk and in large targets, which is where a small book cannot hedge and where screens.md trap 4 applies hardest |
| Jetley & Ji, "The Shrinking Merger Arbitrage Spread: Reasons and Implications", Financial Analysts Journal 66(2), 2010, https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2010/the-shrinking-merger-arbitrage-spread-reasons-and-implications | The merger-arb spread fell by more than 400 bps after 2002, alongside lower aggregate merger-arb hedge fund returns and larger inflows; part of the decline is attributed to lower transaction costs and changed risk and is judged likely permanent | Pre-2002 base rates for spreads and returns are stale; the memo does not quote them. A spread that looks wide against the post-2002 regime is a flag for break risk, not a bargain (trap 1) |
| Lee & Yerramilli, "Specific Performance, Deal Tightness, and Merger Dynamics", working paper, December 2025 (not yet peer-reviewed), https://www.bauer.uh.edu/yerramilli/Merger_Deal_Tightness.pdf | 4,623 SDC-covered deals announced 1994–2022 (merger agreements read for 3,066): only 4.3% of deals fail to complete, 9.5% are renegotiated, 5.2% see a price cut, average time to conclusion 134 days, mean arbitrage spread 5.14%. A deal-tightness index built from specific-performance, MAE-exclusion and bidder-termination provisions predicts fewer failures, faster closing, fewer price cuts and narrower spreads; a strong specific-performance clause largely subsumes the MAE exclusions | Base rates for the memo: failure in the low single digits, renegotiation about twice as common as failure, four to five months to close. Gate: the memo reads the merger agreement and states whether the target has a specific-performance right, how the MAE is carved out, and the reverse termination fee; a deal without specific performance is sized as if the break probability were materially above the base rate |
| Jajal, Mucha, Sweat, Pulman, Flanagan & Anderson, "Global Merger-Arbitrage Forecasting with Language Models", Proceedings of ICML 2026 (peer-reviewed machine-learning conference, not a finance journal), https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09921 | Historical sample of 1,648 public-target deals of roughly US$1bn and above, January 2022 to December 2025: 84.0% closed on announced terms, 2.7% were terminated after a higher bid and 13.7% were terminated with no higher bid. Out-of-sample set of 404 large deals across 42 countries forecast February to December 2025: 84.0% closed as announced; a document-reading model achieves a class-balanced Brier score of 0.151 against 0.199 for Platt-scaled market-implied probabilities and 0.186 for a gradient-boosted baseline, i.e. the spread-implied probability is materially miscalibrated even after calibration | Confirms the Buehlmaier–Zechner rule from the other direction: the spread is not an acceptable source for the break probability. The memo's break estimate comes from the documents (conditions, regulatory stage, financing) and the recent large-deal close rate in the mid-80s percent is the prior, not the US all-deal rate above |

## Lens 5: regulated assets and Brazil
| Paper | Finding | Rule implication |
|---|---|---|

## Proposed amendments

Drafted by the monthly research pass. None is in force. Each becomes policy only when copied, dated and numbered into mandate/amendments.md by the user. The paper each rests on is in the table above.

### P1 (proposed 2026-11-01): merger-arbitrage memo must state deal tightness and a document-based break probability
Rests on Lee & Yerramilli (2025), Jajal et al. (2026), Buehlmaier & Zechner (2021). Change to screens.md lens 2 "Key documents" and to trap 4: the memo states (a) whether the target has a specific-performance right, how the MAE is carved out and the reverse termination fee; (b) a break probability sourced from the documents and credible reporting, never from the spread; (c) the base rates it is departing from: failure in the low single digits of percent for SDC-covered deals 1994–2022 and close-as-announced in the mid-80s percent for large global deals in 2025, with renegotiation about twice as common as failure and four to five months as the typical time to close. A deal with no specific-performance right is sized as if its break probability were materially above the base rate.

### P2 (proposed 2026-11-01): narrow lens 4 index events and fix the holding period
Rests on Greenwood & Sammon (2025), Bektić, Khan & Körber (2025), Murray & Sammon (2026), Escobar et al. (2026), Chang et al. (2025). Change to screens.md lens 4: index additions are never a long after the effective date and may be short or avoid candidates with a holding period of quarters; S&P 500 deletions on their own no longer qualify, because the US large-cap effect has disappeared; qualifying index events are smaller-index, non-US and emerging-market rebalances (MSCI, B3) where the memo can name the mandate-driven flow, and the position is held from announcement to the close of the last day before the effective date, never through it.

### P3 (proposed 2026-11-01): redefine the closer in lens 3
Rests on Bradley et al. (2010), Hardman (2025), ICI (2025, practitioner). Change to screens.md lens 3 "Look at: the closer": "an activist with a track record" qualifies only where the route does not require winning a contested vote (US CEFs with open-ending or tender mechanics the activist can force) or where the board has already announced a discount-control action; for UK investment trusts a contested requisition is not a closer. The target is half the discount, not all of it, and for a tender event the holding period ends at the tender.

### P4 (proposed 2026-11-01): options and shorts under amendment A1
Rests on de Silva, So & Smith (2026), Alexiou et al. (2025), Muravyev, Pearson & Pollet (2025). Additions to A1 item 1: no long option position into a widely anticipated event with elevated implied volatility or a concave near-term volatility curve, which the memo must report before any option trade; option positions are closed on the first session after the event unless the memo re-underwrites them; the 1% default borrow assumption is replaced by the actual fee stated in the memo for every short leg, with the short leg's alpha assumed zero after fees unless the borrow is general-collateral.

### P5 (proposed 2026-11-01): lens 1 and lens 5 holding periods and screen items
Rests on Hotchkiss (1995), Gilson (1997), Kumar et al. (2026, practitioner), Buccola et al. (2026), ABJ Observatório (2022), Araujo et al. (2026). Changes to screens.md lens 1: the memo names pre-filing management continuity as a negative screen item, treats an out-of-court debt-for-equity fix as the higher-risk variant with the 2.5x gate applied without exception, identifies backstop parties from the plan as the expected early sellers, and does not treat a prepack or a clean balance sheet as the thesis. Changes to lens 5 Brazilian special situations: homologação of the plan is not a catalyst; the catalyst is the court's closure of the process, with a slip scenario of two years past homologação, and every RJ memo repeats that no published post-RJ equity base rate exists.

### P6 (proposed 2026-11-04): rights-issue entry window and tradability gate
Rests on Armitage (2007), Eckbo & Masulis (1992), Massa, Mataigne & Vermaelen (2026), Verdú Henares, Carchano Alcina & Ruiz Andújar (2025). Change to screens.md lens 4: the entry point for a rights-issue situation is the subscription period, when pre-renounced shares are placed below the theoretical ex-rights price (around 8% in the UK sample), not after the rump, which clears near market; the sentence "buy the equity ex-rights after the rump" is replaced accordingly. Non-renounceable rights issues at firms without a blockholder are excluded. The screen prefers issues with a high new-to-old share ratio at non-index members and requires stated take-up by insiders and major holders. "Never buy the rights themselves" is unchanged.

### P7 (proposed 2026-11-04): lens 1 holding period, re-filing base rate and rights-offering flag (addendum to P5)
Rests on Eberhart, Altman & Aggarwal (1999), Breuer, Haas & Mersmann (2025), Seth (2025, author summary), Buccola et al. (2026). The default holding period for a post-reorganisation equity is through the second quarterly report after emergence, with a full re-underwrite at that point rather than a multi-year hold by default. The memo carries a re-filing scenario with a base rate of 10% to 25% for a recent large emergence and the top of the reviewed range for a small, still-levered one. An emergence with a backstopped rights offering is flagged, and the entry is not before the backstop lock-up or the first post-emergence quarter, whichever is later.

### P8 (proposed 2026-11-04): closers and deletion reversals (addenda to P2 and P3)
Rests on Kim, Levit & Michaely (2026, working paper), ICI (2026, practitioner), Broby (2026, preprint), Arnott & Henslee (2024, practitioner). Lens 3: a dual-class collapse counts as a closer only at firms older than about twelve years without an existing sunset provision; closed-end fund discount thresholds are relative to the fund's own history and peers, not absolute. Lens 4: a deletion-reversal thesis needs a company-specific path and a twelve-month survival test, because the recent evidence on deletion reversal conflicts.

### P9 (proposed 2026-11-04): category base rates under trap 1
Rests on Chen & Welch (2026, working paper). Addition to screens.md trap 1: a published event anomaly is assumed to earn nothing outside microcaps after 2005, so no memo may cite a category return as the reason the price is wrong; the memo cites the category evidence only to say what it is departing from.
