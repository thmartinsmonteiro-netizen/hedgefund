# Literature: what the evidence says, by lens

Living document. Every entry has a paper, a year, a link, the finding in one line, and what it changes in screens.md. Findings that change no rule do not go in. Entries marked [UNVERIFIED] are recalled from memory and must be replaced by a checked citation or deleted at the next research pass. Last pass: 2026-11-07 (scheduled first-Saturday run: a fourth sweep found one row the three earlier passes lacked, the Jiang, Wang & Yang correction to the lens-1 base rate, and proposes P14; no [UNVERIFIED] rows remain; the 2026-11-06 second supplement: a third sweep adds 13 rows the 2026-11-01 and 2026-11-04 passes did not have and proposes P10 to P13; the 2026-11-04 supplement added rows from a second sweep, corrected the rights-issue entry reading and proposed P6 to P9; the 2026-11-01 pass replaced all seed [UNVERIFIED] rows).

## How the fund uses this file
- The weekly memo cites the entry each candidate leans on, by lens and paper, or writes "no base rate available".
- A finding becomes a rule only through a dated amendment in mandate/amendments.md; this file is evidence, not policy. Candidate rule changes wait under "Proposed amendments" at the bottom until the user adopts or rejects them.
- Base rates from published samples are US-heavy and pre-2020 unless stated; the memo says when the situation is outside the sample (Brazil, Greece, small caps, post-2022 rates).
- Every number in this file appears in the cited paper's own text (abstract, introduction or tables as opened at the pass date). Practitioner sources (indices, vendor reports, trade bodies, blogs) are labelled as such and are pointers, not base rates.

## Lens 0: spin-offs
| Paper | Finding | Rule implication |
|---|---|---|
| Veld & Veld-Merkoulova, "Value creation through spin-offs: a review of the empirical evidence" (meta-analysis of 26 event studies), and their 2004 JBF European study, https://storre.stir.ac.uk/bitstream/1893/1813/1/value-creation-through-spin-offs-a-review-final.pdf | Announcement CAR about +3%; higher for larger, focus-increasing and tax-friendly spin-offs; long-run outperformance found in early US studies disappears with better tests, and European spin-offs show no long-run excess return | The announcement pop is not ours to capture. The lens must be about post-separation forced selling and coverage gaps, not about "spin-offs outperform". Prefer focus-increasing separations; do not assume a category base rate for Europe or LatAm |
| Aranca special report on the S&P U.S. Spin-Off Index, June 2026, https://www.aranca.com/assets/docs/Special_Report_Spin-Offs_Where_Value_Accrues_After_Separation.pdf | Index outperformed the S&P 500 by roughly 15 points over three years to mid-2026, almost all from mid-2025 onward; industrials dominate the opportunity set | A practitioner index, not a paper; treat as a pointer. Recent outperformance means the category is crowded now (trap 1); the bar for "why is this specific one mispriced" goes up, not down |
| Cusatis, Miles & Woolridge, "Restructuring through spinoffs: The stock market evidence", Journal of Financial Economics 33(3), 1993, https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/restructuring-through-spinoffs-the-stock-market-evidence/ ; and McConnell, Ozbilgin & Wahal, "Spin-Offs, Ex Ante", Journal of Business 74(2), 2001, https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:ucp:jnlbus:v:74:y:2001:i:2:p:245-80 | CMW find significantly positive abnormal returns for spinoffs, parents and the combination over up to three years after separation (1965–1988), but the abnormal performance is limited to firms involved in takeover activity; spinoffs and parents are taken over unusually often. McConnell et al. test the CMW strategy ex ante over 1989–95 and find it does not beat a matched-firm benchmark or the Fama–French three-factor model, though it beats size/book-to-market matched portfolios | The post-spin "drift" is a takeover-candidacy effect, not a category base rate, and it failed out of sample. A lens-0 memo must name the specific forced seller and, where the thesis is re-rating rather than flow, must argue why the spin or the parent is a plausible control target; otherwise "no base rate available" |
| Lim & Choi, "When 'Split' Is Not Enough: Hedge Fund Activism and the Long-Run Performance of Spin-offs", SSRN working paper, posted May 2026 (not peer-reviewed), https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6735651 | Hand-collected sample: hedge-fund-led spin-offs do not outperform, and if anything underperform, manager-led ones in the long run, with little evidence of the expected gains in focus or governance for either parent or spinco | An activist having forced the separation is not a plus in the screen. Treat "activist-driven split" as neutral at best; the memo must show an operating or governance change beyond the boundary change itself |
| Houlihan Lokey, "Fall 2025 Spin-Off Transaction Study" (practitioner, investment bank), data to 2025-06-30, https://cdn.hl.com/pdf/2025/spin-off-transaction-study-fall-2025-hl.pdf | 73 US spin-offs closed Jan 2019 to Jun 2025: only 40% of SpinCos beat the S&P 500 at one year (median excess -6.7%) and 42% at 18 months (median -12.1%); RemainCos did worse (25% ahead at one year, median -12.0%; 35% at 18 months, median -16.9%); the combined SpinCo plus RemainCo from the day before announcement to 30 days after the effective date beat the index 52% of the time (median +2.3%) | The 2019 to 2025 cohort does not support the screens.md line that the parent "often re-rates later than the spin". A parent-after-disposal idea needs a named post-disposal mechanism with a date or it is a watchlist entry (P10). Read with the Aranca row: the recent index outperformance is a few large winners, not a cohort effect |

A prepack is not a plus. | Seth, "Do rights offerings reduce bargaining complexity in Chapter 11?", Oxford Business Law Blog, May 2025, https://blogs.law.ox.ac.uk/oblb/blog-post/2025/05/do-rights-offerings-reduce-bargaining-complexity-chapter-11 (author summary of SSRN 4250057, which could not be opened; not peer-reviewed) | Large listed Chapter 11s 2003-2021: rights offerings appear in 35% of cases by assets (87% in 2019); backstop participants earned about 50% within three months of emergence; hedge funds bought about 43% of reorganised equity and appointed about 40% of boards; post-bankruptcy equity of rights-offering cases outperformed other emergers by about 30% | Complements Buccola et al.: the backstop group's gain is realised in the first three months, so that is when their selling arrives. | AllianceBernstein, "Merger arbitrage: riding the wave into 2026", Dec 2025 (practitioner, not a paper), https://www.alliancebernstein.com/us/en-us/investments/insights/investment-insights/merger-arbitrage-riding-the-wave-into-2026.html | HFRI merger-arb index +8.2% through Sep 2025; returns came from spread compression as regulatory risk fell | Context only: a benign regulatory regime compresses spreads, so the residual wide spreads are the ones with real break risk (trap 4) |
| Mitchell & Pulvino, "Characteristics of Risk and Return in Risk Arbitrage", Journal of Finance 56(6), 2001, https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v56y2001i6p2135-2175.html ; Baker & Savasoglu, "Limited arbitrage in mergers and acquisitions", Journal of Financial Economics 64(1), 2002, https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/357797 (abstract mirror; journal record https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v64y2002i1p91-115.html) | Mitchell & Pulvino, 4,750 mergers 1963–1998: returns are uncorrelated with the market in flat and rising markets and positively correlated in severely falling ones, i.e. the payoff of a written index put; after the non-linearity and transaction costs, excess returns of about four percent a year. Sizing is for the break, and the memo states what the position does in a severe market drawdown, when breaks cluster. The Baker–Savasoglu result says the premium sits in the deals with visible completion risk and in large targets, which is where a small book cannot hedge and where screens.md trap 4 applies hardest |
| Jetley & Ji, "The Shrinking Merger Arbitrage Spread: Reasons and Implications", Financial Analysts Journal 66(2), 2010, https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2010/the-shrinking-merger-arbitrage-spread-reasons-and-implications | The merger-arb spread fell by more than 400 bps after 2002, alongside lower aggregate merger-arb hedge fund returns and larger inflows; part of the decline is attributed to lower transaction costs and changed risk and is judged likely permanent | Pre-2002 base rates for spreads and returns are stale; the memo does not quote them. A spread that looks wide against the post-2002 regime is a flag for break risk, not a bargain (trap 1) |
| Lee & Yerramilli, "Specific Performance, Deal Tightness, and Merger Dynamics", working paper, December 2025 (not yet peer-reviewed), https://www.bauer.uh.edu/yerramilli/Merger_Deal_Tightness.pdf | 4,623 SDC-covered deals announced 1994–2022 (merger agreements read for 3,066): only 4.3% of deals fail to complete, 9.5% are renegotiated, 5.2% see a price cut, average time to conclusion 134 days, mean arbitrage spread 5.14%. A deal-tightness index built from specific-performance, MAE-exclusion and bidder-termination provisions predicts fewer failures, faster closing, fewer price cuts and narrower spreads; a strong specific-performance clause largely subsumes the MAE exclusions | Base rates for the memo: failure in the low single digits, renegotiation about twice as common as failure, four to five months to close. Gate: the memo reads the merger agreement and states whether the target has a specific-performance right, how the MAE is carved out, and the reverse termination fee; a deal without specific performance is sized as if the break probability were materially above the base rate |
| Jajal, Mucha, Sweat, Pulman, Flanagan & Anderson, "Global Merger-Arbitrage Forecasting with Language Models", Proceedings of ICML 2026 (peer-reviewed machine-learning conference, not a finance journal), https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09921 | Historical sample of 1,648 public-target deals of roughly US$1bn and above, January 2022 to December 2025: 84.0% closed on announced terms, 2.7% were terminated after a higher bid and 13.7% were terminated with no higher bid. | Davidson Kempner, "Merger arbitrage: Opportunity in regulatory complexity", July 2026 (practitioner, asset manager), https://saprdeus90c0a106.b-cdn.net/35799a9a-8242-4d29-ad61-238367edb583/DK_MAOpportunityinRegulatoryComplexity_Final.pdf | Since 2021, deals with 100 to 220 days left to close that face a long regulatory path traded at median spreads about 180 bp wider than simple deals (about 50 bp wider in 2016 to 2020); average EU review of mega deals rose from 175 days (2001 to 2015) to 254 days, US from 198 to 229; the share of deals above US$10bn disclosing an expected foreign-investment review rose from 11% (2005 to 2020) to 40% (2021 to 1H26); CADE blocked 14 of about 7,000 deals since 2012 (citing a 2025 Tavares de Araujo report) | Trap 6 made quantitative for the current regime: the slip scenario in a lens-2 memo uses current review timelines (about 8 months EU, 7.5 months US for large deals), not the agreement's outside date, and prices the time as well as the probability. For a Brazilian target a CADE block is rare but a negotiated remedy means a long review (P11) |

## Lens 3: holdcos and closed-end funds
| Paper | Finding | Rule implication |
|---|---|---|
| Lee, Shleifer & Thaler, "Investor Sentiment and the Closed-End Fund Puzzle", Journal of Finance 46(1), 1991 (NBER WP 3465), https://www.nber.org/papers/w3465 | Closed-end fund discounts move together, new funds launch when existing funds trade at premiums or small discounts, and discounts narrow when small stocks do well; discounts are read as a measure of individual-investor sentiment | A wide discount with no closer is a sentiment position, not a special situation; it can widen for years. Confirms the screens.md rule that only a discount with a mechanism qualifies, and says the discount level alone is not the mechanism |
| Bradley, Brav, Goldstein & Jiang, "Activist arbitrage: A study of open-ending attempts of closed-end funds", Journal of Financial Economics 95(1), 2010, https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:95:y:2010:i:1:p:1-19 | Open-ending attempts substantially reduce discounts, on average to half their original level; whether a fund is targeted depends on the size of the discount, shareholder-communication costs and the fund's governance structure | The closer that works is a credible open-ending or tender attempt, and it works partially: the memo targets half the discount, not all of it, and checks governance (staggered board, supermajority, control-share provisions) before counting an activist as a closer. The working-paper text (https://rodneywhitecenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/0623.pdf, 142 funds 1988 to 2002) adds that attempts occurred in 13.3% of fund-years and actual open-endings in 3.9%, that targeted funds had discounts 6 to 7 points wider than peers in the years before an attempt, and that 60.6% of actual open-endings came only after dissidents won board control: the activist must be able to win a proxy fight, and the prize the evidence supports is the gap to peers, not the whole discount |
| Cornell & Liu, "The Parent Company Puzzle: When is the Whole Worth Less than One of the Parts?", Journal of Corporate Finance 7(4), 2001 (working-paper version opened), https://escholarship.org/content/qt1sv5f5xf/qt1sv5f5xf.pdf | Seven cases where a parent's market value was below the value of its listed stake; tax, agency, liquidity and noise-trader explanations fail, leaving mispricing of the subsidiary's scarce shares; control transactions to exploit the gap were launched in five of the seven pairs | Negative-stub holdcos are rare and the gap closes through a transaction, so the position is on the parent and the memo names the transaction (distribution, exchange offer, buy-in) and who can trigger it. Dual-class unification evidence was searched for and not opened this pass; no row |
| Hardman, "Saba rattling in UK investment trusts: Three lessons for capital market regulation", European Business Organization Law Review, accepted September 2025 (peer-reviewed law journal), https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/saba-rattling-in-uk-investment-trusts-three-lessons-for-capital-m/ | Saba built stakes in seven UK investment trusts, requisitioned meetings at all seven in December 2024 to replace boards and open-end them, and lost all seven; incumbent boards campaigned with company resources and retail platform ownership made activist vote mobilisation structurally hard | For UK trusts, "activist with a track record" is not a sufficient closer when the route is a contested vote. The memo requires a board-sponsored discount-control action (tender, buyback at NAV, wind-down or merger vote) already announced, or treats the position as a paper watchlist entry |
| Investment Company Institute, "Closed-End Fund Activism", 2025 (trade body; interested party; data to December 2024), https://www.ici.org/files/2025/cef-activism.pdf | Activists held shares in 44 percent of all US CEFs as of December 2024; across 44 forced tender offers between 2015 and June 2023, nearly half of activists exited the fund within three months of the tender; the number of listed CEFs fell 43 percent since 2007 | Practitioner and advocacy source, but the exit pattern is the useful part: for a US CEF tender event the holding period ends at the tender, and the memo does not assume the discount stays narrow after the activist leaves |
| Investment Company Institute, "The closed-end fund market, 2025", Research Perspective 32(3), April 2026 (trade body; interested party), https://www.ici.org/system/files/2026-04/per-32-03.pdf | US equity CEFs averaged a 7.0% discount at end-2024 and 5.6% at end-2025; 364 traditional CEFs at end-2025 against 634 in 2011; 25 Schedule 13D or contested filings on 24 funds in 2025; activists completely exited 72% of the funds where they won tender offers between January 2015 and July 2025 | Updates the row above with 2025 data: discounts are narrow by history, so the screen uses the discount relative to the fund's own history and peers rather than an absolute level, and the 72% exit rate confirms that the holding period of an activist-driven CEF position ends at the tender |
| Investment Company Institute, "Confronting the growing burden of fund proxy campaigns", March 2026 (trade body; interested party), https://www.ici.org/system/files/2026-03/26-confronting-growing-burden-fund-proxy-campaigns.pdf | 58 activist-forced tender offers Jan 2015 to Jul 2025: 72% of activists sold within one year and 48% within three months; for the targeted funds the average excess discount was 5.3% in the year before the activist's first filing, 0.5% during the tender period and 3.1% in the year after; listed CEFs fell from 658 (2007) to 382 (2025) | Gives the shape of the prize the two ICI rows above only imply: about 5 points of excess discount close into the tender and more than half of that reopens within a year. | Kim, Levit & Michaely, "The dynamic trade-off of dual-class shares", arXiv 2608.25972 / ECGI Finance WP 590, August 2026 (working paper), https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25972 | US dual-class panel 1971-2022 with 224 unifications: Tobin's q falls initially after unification and recovers toward the pre-event level by about five years; among 61 unification announcements the CAR is 5.2 points higher for firms older than 12 years; unifying has no valuation effect at firms that already had sunset provisions | Fills the gap noted in the Cornell & Liu row: a dual-class collapse counts as a closer only at mature firms (older than about 12 years) without an existing sunset; elsewhere the memo gets no credit for the unification and must not price a permanent re-rating |

## Proposed amendments

Drafted by the monthly research pass. None is in force. Each becomes policy only when copied, dated and numbered into mandate/amendments.md by the user. The paper each rests on is in the table above.

### P1 (proposed 2026-11-01): merger-arbitrage memo must state deal tightness and a document-based break probability
Rests on Lee & Yerramilli (2025), Jajal et al. (2026), Buehlmaier & Zechner (2021). Change to screens.md lens 2 "Key documents" and to trap 4: the memo states (a) whether the target has a specific-performance right, how the MAE is carved out and the reverse termination fee; (b) a break probability sourced from the documents and credible reporting, never from the spread; (c) the base rates it is departing from: failure in the low single digits of percent for SDC-covered deals 1994–2022 and close-as-announced in the mid-80s percent for large global deals in 2025, with renegotiation about twice as common as failure and four to five months as the typical time to close. A deal with no specific-performance right is sized as if its break probability were materially above the base rate.

### P2 (proposed 2026-11-01): narrow lens 4 index events and fix the holding period
Rests on Greenwood & Sammon (2025), Bektić, Khan & Körber (2025), Murray & Sammon (2026), Escobar et al. (2026), Chang et al. (2025). Change to screens.md lens 4: index additions are never a long after the effective date and may be short or avoid candidates with a holding period of quarters; S&P 500 deletions on their own no longer qualify, because the US large-cap effect has disappeared; qualifying index events are smaller-index, non-US and emerging-market rebalances (MSCI, B3) where the memo can name the mandate-driven flow, and the position is held from announcement to the close of the last day before the effective date, never through it.

### P3 (proposed 2026-11-01): redefine the closer in lens 3
Rests on Bradley et al. (2010), Hardman (2025), ICI (2025, practitioner). Change to screens.md lens 3 "Look at: the closer": "an activist with a track record" qualifies only where the route does not require winning a contested vote (US CEFs with open-ending or tender mechanics the activist can force) or where the board has already announced a discount-control action; for UK investment trusts a contested requisition is not a closer. The target is half the discount, not all of it, and for a tender event the holding period ends at the tender.

### P4 (proposed 2026-11-01): options and shorts under amendment A1
Rests on de Silva, So & Smith (2026), Alexiou et al. (2025), Muravyev, Pearson & Pollet (2025). Additions to A1 item 1: no long option position into a widely anticipated event with elevated implied volatility or a concave near-term volatility curve, which the memo must report before any option trade; option positions are closed on the first session after the event unless the memo re-underwrites them; the 1% default borrow assumption is replaced by the actual fee stated in the memo for every short leg, with the short leg's alpha assumed zero after fees unless the borrow is general-collateral.

### P5 (proposed 2026-11-01): lens 1 and lens 5 holding periods and screen items
Rests on Hotchkiss (1995), Gilson (1997), Kumar et al. (2026, practitioner), Buccola et al. (2026), ABJ Observatório (2022), Araujo et al. (2026). Changes to screens.md lens 1: the memo names pre-filing management continuity as a negative screen item, treats an out-of-court debt-for-equity fix as the higher-risk variant with the 2.5x gate applied without exception, identifies backstop parties from the plan as the expected early sellers, and does not treat a prepack or a clean balance sheet as the thesis. Changes to lens 5 Brazilian special situations: homologação of the plan is not a catalyst; the catalyst is the court's closure of the process, with a slip scenario of two years past homologação, and every RJ memo repeats that no published post-RJ equity base rate exists.

### P6 (proposed 2026-11-04): rights-issue entry window and tradability gate
Rests on Armitage (2007), Eckbo & Masulis (1992), Massa, Mataigne & Vermaelen (2026), Verdú Henares, Carchano Alcina & Ruiz Andújar (2025). Change to screens.md lens 4: the entry point for a rights-issue situation is the subscription period, when pre-renounced shares are placed below the theoretical ex-rights price (around 8% in the UK sample), not after the rump, which clears near market; the sentence "buy the equity ex-rights after the rump" is replaced accordingly. Non-renounceable rights issues at firms without a blockholder are excluded. The screen prefers issues with a high new-to-old share ratio at non-index members and requires stated take-up by insiders and major holders. "Never buy the rights themselves" is unchanged.

### P7 (proposed 2026-11-04): lens 1 holding period, re-filing base rate and rights-offering flag (addendum to P5)
Rests on Eberhart, Altman & Aggarwal (1999), Breuer, Haas & Mersmann (2025), Seth (2025, author summary), Buccola et al. (2026). The default holding period for a post-reorganisation equity is through the second quarterly report after emergence, with a full re-underwrite at that point rather than a multi-year hold by default. The memo carries a re-filing scenario with a base rate of 10% to 25% for a recent large emergence and the top of the reviewed range for a small, still-levered one. An emergence with a backstopped rights offering is flagged, and the entry is not before the backstop lock-up or the first post-emergence quarter, whichever is later.

### P8 (proposed 2026-11-04): closers and deletion reversals (addenda to P2 and P3)
Rests on Kim, Levit & Michaely (2026, working paper), ICI (2026, practitioner), Broby (2026, preprint), Arnott & Henslee (2024, practitioner). Lens 3: a dual-class collapse counts as a closer only at firms older than about twelve years without an existing sunset provision; closed-end fund discount thresholds are relative to the fund's own history and peers, not absolute. Lens 4: a deletion-reversal thesis needs a company-specific path and a twelve-month survival test, because the recent evidence on deletion reversal conflicts.

### P9 (proposed 2026-11-04): category base rates under trap 1
Rests on Chen & Welch (2026, working paper). Addition to screens.md trap 1: a published event anomaly is assumed to earn nothing outside microcaps after 2005, so no memo may cite a category return as the reason the price is wrong; the memo cites the category evidence only to say what it is departing from.

### P10 (proposed 2026-11-06): parent-after-disposal demoted to watchlist
