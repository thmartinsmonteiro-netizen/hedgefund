## The single most important structural fact

**The human capital is already 100% long Brazil and long the private equity cycle.**

Salary, bonus, carry, and job security all depend on the same handful of factors: Brazilian
GDP, the Selic rate, BRL, local credit availability, and appetite for illiquid risk. A
personal portfolio that is also long Brazilian equities, Brazilian credit, levered small-caps
and cyclical financials is not a diversified life — it is a concentrated bet placed twice.

Therefore:

- **Brazil equity target: 0–10% of the portfolio.** Not because Brazil is a bad market, but
  because the household is already saturated with the exposure and cannot add more without
  raising total risk far more than it raises expected return.
- **Avoid the satellite loading up on** highly levered companies, banks and specialty lenders,
  commercial real estate, BDCs, listed alternative asset managers, and anything whose fortunes
  turn on cheap financing. Those are the same risk factor that pays the salary.
- Assets that do well when Brazil does badly (hard currency, global defensives) are worth more
  to this investor than their standalone Sharpe ratio suggests.

## Structure: the currency and domicile layer

Brazil has **no income tax treaty with the United States.** This has two consequences that
matter more than most stock picks.

1. **Dividend withholding.** US-domiciled ETFs suffer 30% US withholding on dividends for a
   Brazilian holder. An Ireland-domiciled UCITS ETF holding the same US stocks suffers 15% at
   the fund level (US–Ireland treaty) and 0% Irish withholding out to a non-resident. On an
   S&P 500 tracker that difference is roughly **0.2 percentage points per year, forever.**
2. **US estate tax.** US-situs assets — US-domiciled ETFs *and* individual US-listed stocks —
   above a **US$60,000** exemption are exposed to US estate tax at rates up to **40%** for a
   non-resident alien with no estate tax treaty. Ireland-domiciled UCITS ETFs are not US-situs
   and sidestep this entirely.

**Rules that follow:**

- The **core is held exclusively in Ireland-domiciled, accumulating UCITS ETFs** (typically
  LSE- or Euronext-listed, USD or GBP line). Accumulating share classes also avoid a stream of
  distributions to track and reinvest.
- Confirm with IBKR that the specific UCITS lines are tradeable in the account. Access varies
  by entity and client classification; check before building the plan around a ticker.
- Individual **US-listed satellite names remain US-situs.** This is accepted for now given
  size and age, but total US single-name exposure is worth watching, and non-US listings of
  otherwise equivalent businesses are mildly preferred, all else equal.

## Tax treatment (Brazil)

Under **Lei 14.754/2023**, income from financial investments abroad held by a Brazilian
individual is taxed at a **flat 15%**, assessed **annually** in the Declaração de Ajuste Anual,
separately from other income. Losses within the bucket may be offset. The old monthly
capital-gains regime with its DARF-by-the-month rhythm no longer governs these investments.

Practical implication: **rebalancing and trimming are cheap in friction terms.** There is no
monthly tax filing consequence to a mid-year trade. This is a meaningful advantage over the
old regime and the mandate can afford real discipline on position limits.

Foreign currency held as cash is a separate line with its own USD 5,000 annual exemption and
progressive rates.

*A contador who handles offshore reporting should confirm the specifics for the actual account
structure. The agent flags tax-relevant events; it does not file anything.*

## Decision cadence — weekly research, bi-monthly decisions

**Research runs weekly. Buying decisions do not.**

The weekly memo monitors, screens and builds theses. But a new satellite idea can only be
*bought* at a **decision gate**, which falls at the end of each 8-week lens cycle — roughly every
two months, aligned with lens 6 (portfolio week) and lens 7 (anti-thesis week).

An idea surfaced in week 33 is therefore bought no earlier than week 39. It has to survive
several weeks of you looking at it, plus a deliberate attempt to destroy it in anti-thesis week.
Most ideas do not survive that, which is the entire point: **the cooling-off period is the
filter.** Enthusiasm decays faster than a good thesis does.

Three exceptions where action is allowed between gates:
1. **A kill criterion trips** on an existing holding — sell or trim immediately, no waiting.
2. **Scheduled core contributions** — these are automatic and never discretionary.
3. **Fast sleeve trades** — by construction these have short-dated catalysts and cannot wait.

## The fast sleeve

You asked for a place to run 1–2 month trades. This is it, and it is deliberately walled off.

**Rules:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Starting size | 5% of invested assets |
| Hard cap | 10% — if it grows past this, the excess moves to the core |
| Max positions | 3–5 at once |
| Position horizon | 1–8 weeks, defined at entry |
| Required at entry | **A dated catalyst.** You must name the specific event and the date it resolves — earnings, M&A close, index rebalance, spin-off completion, regulatory decision, contract award. "It looks cheap" is not a catalyst. |
| Liquidity | Liquid names only. This sleeve needs a fast exit, so the ADV test is stricter here: position ≤ 5% of one day's volume |
| Exit | **Mandatory at the catalyst date**, win or lose. No letting winners run — that converts a trade into an accidental investment and destroys the measurement. |
| Leverage, options | Still none |

**The ring-fence.** The fast sleeve is **never topped up from the core or satellite.** If it
loses money, it stays small and you trade a smaller book. It grows only from its own profits or
from a fixed, pre-agreed slice of new contributions. This is the rule that makes it a contained
experiment rather than a leak.

**The sunset clause.** Every trade is logged at entry with the prediction, the catalyst, the date
and the reasoning. Quarterly, the sleeve is scored against the honest benchmark: *what if this
money had simply sat in the core ETF?* — after commissions, spreads, FX and the 15% tax.

**After four quarters, if the fast sleeve has not beaten that benchmark, it closes and the capital
moves to the core.** Pre-committed, decided now while it costs nothing to be objective.

This is not pessimism about your ability. It is the only design in which you actually find out.
Most people who trade never learn whether they are good at it, because they never define the
benchmark in advance and never write down the prediction before the outcome is known. You will
have both, and either answer is worth having: if it works, you scale it with evidence; if it does
not, you found out with 5% instead of 50%.

## Build order — starting from zero

As of August 2026 there is **nothing invested abroad.** The target allocation above is the
destination, not the starting point. Trying to run a 15-name satellite book on a small base is
the most reliable way to turn a good process into a bad outcome: fixed costs eat small orders,
each position is too small to matter, and the attention required is the same as it would be on
ten times the capital.

The sleeves get built in order. **Do not start phase 2 early.**

### Phase 1 — Core only (expect 12–18 months)
Fund the IBKR account on the quarterly cadence. Contributions go into 1–2 Ireland-domiciled
accumulating UCITS ETFs. Nothing else. No single names.

During this phase the weekly memo still runs — but its job changes. Instead of proposing trades
it: tracks the FX and remittance cost of each transfer, confirms contributions happened, runs
the lens as a **paper exercise** so the research muscle develops, and maintains a watchlist with
written theses that are never acted on. Twelve months of forecasts you can check against reality,
with no money at risk, is the cheapest tuition available.

### Phase 2 — Satellite opens
Begin single names when the offshore portfolio is large enough that a position clears the
US$1,000 minimum without breaching the 8% cap — around US$12,500 invested. Ramp deliberately:
3 positions in the first year of phase 2, 6 as the book grows, judged on whether the written
paper theses played out, not on whether the portfolio went up.

The **fast sleeve also opens at phase 2**, not before — and paper-trades throughout phase 1. A
year of logged paper catalyst trades, scored against the core, tells you whether the real sleeve
is worth opening at all. That record costs nothing and is the most valuable input to the decision.

### The reason for the wait
The satellite sleeve has to beat a global index fund after costs, FX, taxes and your time. On a
base too small, fixed costs eat the answer before it becomes visible. The core compounds either
way, and the paper record builds the evidence for free.

## Hard limits

These are not guidelines. A proposed trade that breaks one of these does not go in the memo.

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| Max single satellite position, at cost | 8% of portfolio |
| Trim trigger, on appreciation | 12% of portfolio |
| Max number of satellite positions | 6 |
| Min position size | **US$1,000 absolute.** Below this a ~US$5 commission is >0.5% on entry alone, and 1%+ round-trip before the thesis does anything |
| Min liquidity | Position ≤ 15% of one day's average traded value, **and** ADV ≥ US$150,000 |
| Max satellite exposure to one sector | 25% of the satellite sleeve |
| Max satellite exposure to one country | 40% of the satellite sleeve |
| Max new capital deployed per decision gate | 6% of portfolio |
| Fast sleeve, total | 10% of invested assets, hard cap, never topped up from other sleeves |
| Satellite buys between decision gates | None, except kill-criterion sells |
| Brazil equity | 0–10% of total portfolio |
| Leverage / margin | None |
| Options, futures, crypto | Not in mandate |

**On the 15-position cap:** this is a constraint on *attention*, not on diversification. A
full-time private equity job leaves a few hours a week for this. Fifteen names is already
optimistic. A portfolio of 30 half-followed positions is worse than an index fund, because it
has the fees and the risk without the knowledge.

## Kill criteria discipline

Every satellite position is entered with 2–3 written, observable exit triggers, recorded in
`holdings.md` at purchase. Written *before* buying, when judgment is clear. The point is to
make the sell decision at the moment of least emotional interference.

Good kill criterion: "organic revenue growth below 3% for two consecutive quarters" or
"net debt / EBITDA above 3.5x" or "the founder-CEO leaves."

Bad kill criterion: "the thesis stops working" or "down 30%." Price is not information.

## Review cadence

- **Weekly** — the memo. Thesis maintenance plus one research lens.
- **Quarterly** — rebalance back to bands if breached; review every kill criterion for whether
  it is still the right criterion.
- **Annually** — revisit this mandate. Income and contribution capacity will have changed.
  So will the appropriate risk.

## Contribution mechanics

Confirmed 13 Aug 2026: **R$3,000/month** available; IBKR account open and working.

**Remit quarterly, not monthly.** IOF on an investment remittance to a same-owner account is
**1.10%**, and FX spread is percentage-based — neither changes with frequency. Broker commission
is a *fixed* amount per order, so it is the whole argument:

| Cadence | Per remittance | Total friction | Annual cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly | ~US$556 | **2.50%** | ~US$167 |
| **Quarterly** | ~US$1,667 | **1.90%** | ~US$127 |
| Semi-annual | ~US$3,333 | 1.75% | ~US$117 |
| Annual | ~US$6,667 | 1.68% | ~US$112 |

Quarterly captures most of the saving. Going less frequent than quarterly saves another ~US$15/yr
and costs an average of extra months out of the market — not worth it. Idle BRL waits in Tesouro
Selic, so batching costs nothing in the meantime.

**Sizing consequence.** At R$3,000/month the portfolio reaches roughly US$6.6k after year one and
US$20k after year three. A 3%-of-portfolio position would be US$200–600 — a US$5 commission is
1–2.5% round-trip before anything else happens. This is why the position limits above were
rewritten to **6 positions at up to 8%**, with a hard **US$1,000 minimum**, rather than 15 at 3%.
Concentration here is a cost constraint, not a conviction statement.

Practically: **the satellite opens at around US$12,500 invested** — roughly 2 years of
contributions, sooner with market growth. Until then it is core ETFs plus paper-tracking.
