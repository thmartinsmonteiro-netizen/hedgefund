# Screens and the Weekly Lens Rotation (Special Situations)

Last updated: 2026-09-09. Replaces the "under-covered" version of 2026-08-12. The mandate is
unchanged; this file changes only *where the agent looks* and *what counts as a candidate*.

## The bias, stated operationally

The satellite and fast sleeve hunt for **special situations**: positions where the expected
return comes from an identifiable event, structure or forced flow, not from a general view that
a business is good or a stock is cheap. The edge, if there is one, is doing the document work
that most holders of the security did not do.

A candidate must have **all three** of the following, and the memo must name each explicitly:

| Element | What it means |
|---|---|
| **A defined event or structural feature** | Something specific is happening or already happened: a separation, a reorganisation, a tender, a liquidation, a regulatory decision, a rights issue, a holdco collapse. "Cheap" is not an event. |
| **A reason the price is wrong** | Who is the forced or uninformed seller, and why? Index exclusion, mandate constraints, creditor-turned-shareholder, complexity nobody priced, a filing nobody read. If the answer is "the market is dumb", the idea does not go in the memo. |
| **A path and a date by which the gap should close** | Not a hope. A mechanism (completion, distribution, conversion, re-rating on first clean quarter) and a date the memo can be held to. |

"Under-covered" remains a **tiebreaker**, not a screen: between two situations of similar
quality, prefer the one with fewer than five analysts and no index sponsorship.

## Non-negotiable gates

Unchanged from the mandate. Every candidate must clear all of these before it can appear in
the memo:

- **Liquidity, scaled to position size:** the intended position must be **≤ 15% of one day's
  average traded value**, and the name must trade at least **US$150,000/day** in absolute
  terms. Being right and unable to sell is the same as being wrong.
- **Tradeable at IBKR** on an exchange the account can reach, in a currency it can settle.
  Post-reorg equities that trade OTC or on the pink sheets fail this unless IBKR confirms
  access; when-issued lines are not tradeable in this account.
- **Filings in English or Portuguese**, or with a reliable translated annual report. Special
  situations live in the documents; if you cannot read the plan of reorganisation, the
  information statement or the offer document, you cannot underwrite the situation.
- **Audited by a firm that exists**, with no auditor resignation in the last two years.
- **Not a shell, SPAC, pre-revenue biotech, or single-asset mining explorer.**
- **Positive operating cash flow** in at least two of the last three fiscal years. Exception
  allowed only for post-reorganisation equities, and only where the memo shows post-emergence
  cash generation from the disclosure statement's projections and the first reported quarter.
- **The mandate's factor exclusion applies to special situations too.** No highly levered
  companies, banks, specialty lenders, commercial real estate, BDCs, or listed alternative
  managers in the satellite. This closes off a lot of distressed equity. That is deliberate:
  those are the exposures that already pay the salary. A post-reorg equity qualifies only if
  net debt / EBITDA at emergence is below 2.5x and the memo shows it.
- **Brazil cap: 0 to 10% of total portfolio.** Brazilian recuperação judicial exits are a
  legitimate lens (see lens 5) but the whole Brazilian sleeve, including any B3-listed name,
  sits inside that cap.

## Instruments

Equities and equity-like securities only: common stock, post-reorg common, listed warrants
received in a reorganisation (hold, never buy), rights received in a rights issue (exercise
or sell, never buy), listed holding companies, closed-end funds and investment trusts at a
discount. **No options, futures, margin or crypto** per the mandate.

**Credit is paper-only.** Distressed bonds and loans are the natural home of a special
situations mandate and are out of reach at this portfolio size: minimum lots, IBKR bond
access, and the salary-correlated credit factor all argue against it. The agent may write up
a credit situation as a paper position in the watchlist, clearly labelled, to build the
record that the Starboard version will need. It never proposes buying one.

## The traps, stated plainly

1. **The event is already priced.** Merger arb spreads, spin-off dips and post-reorg
   discounts are the most studied pockets in the market. The memo must show why the specific
   situation is mispriced, not why the category historically worked.
2. **The forced seller is also the informed seller.** A creditor who received equity in a
   reorganisation and is selling it may simply know the business. Distinguish mandate-driven
   selling (index fund, credit fund that cannot hold equity) from view-driven selling.
3. **Complexity is not the same as opportunity.** A situation with six moving parts is easier
   to be wrong about, and a small portfolio cannot hedge the parts it does not want.
4. **Tail risk is the whole trade.** In merger arb and tenders the downside is the pre-deal
   price, often 20 to 40% below. Size for the break, not the spread. Regulatory and financing
   risk must be argued at equal length to the spread.
5. **Governance risk scales with distress.** Controlling shareholders, DIP lenders and
   plan sponsors write the documents. Read the plan for what the minority actually gets.
6. **Dated catalysts slip.** Completions, court confirmations and regulatory decisions are
   routinely delayed. Every catalyst date in a memo carries a slip scenario and what the
   position does if the date moves by two quarters.
7. **You have a day job and this research is document-heavy.** Be honest each week about
   whether you actually read the filings or read about them.

## The weekly lens rotation

Allocation and rules never change week to week. What changes is where you go looking.
Lens = `ISO week number mod 8`. Lenses 6 and 7 are the decision gate, unchanged.

### 0 — Spin-offs, demergers and carve-outs
Companies recently separated from a parent, and parents that have just shed a division.
Forced selling by index funds and by holders who wanted the parent. Coverage often drops to
zero for two to four quarters.
Look at: spin-offs 1 to 18 months post-separation, the *parent* after a large disposal
(often re-rates later than the spin), partial IPOs of subsidiaries, and Form 10-12B or
prospectus filings for separations that have not completed yet (watchlist only).
Key document: the information statement or prospectus. Read the pro forma balance sheet and
the management incentive plan before anything else.

### 1 — Post-reorganisation and post-distress equities
Companies that emerged from Chapter 11, a UK scheme or administration, or an equivalent
process, within the last 24 months, plus companies that completed a large equity raise or
debt-for-equity swap to fix the balance sheet without a court process.
Look at: no coverage, creditor-turned-shareholder registers, a first clean reporting cycle,
and a fresh-start balance sheet nobody has modelled. The gate above on leverage at emergence
applies; the memo shows net debt / EBITDA from the emergence balance sheet.
Key documents: the plan of reorganisation, the disclosure statement projections, the first
10-Q or equivalent after emergence.

### 2 — Merger arbitrage and tender offers
Announced, definitive-agreement transactions: cash or stock mergers, tender offers, mandatory
offers, squeeze-outs, take-privates by controlling shareholders.
Look at: spreads wider than the risk-free rate plus a credible break probability; minority
squeeze-outs where the offer is below appraised value; offers with a pending bump. Stock
deals are watchlist only, since the mandate has no shorting to hedge the acquirer.
This lens feeds the **fast sleeve** by construction: a dated close is a named catalyst. Sizing
is for the break scenario. The memo states the downside to the unaffected price, the
regulatory path (which authorities, which stage), the financing condition and the outside
date.
Key documents: the merger agreement (conditions, outside date, reverse termination fee), the
proxy or offer document, and regulatory filings.

### 3 — Holding companies, closed-end funds and sum-of-the-parts
Listed holdcos trading at a discount to their listed stakes, investment trusts and closed-end
funds at wide discounts to NAV, companies with cash or a listed stake approaching a large
share of market cap, dual-class structures about to collapse.
Look at: the *closer*, not the discount. Most holdco discounts persist for decades. Only a
discount with a mechanism to narrow qualifies: an announced distribution or spin of the stake,
a buyback at NAV, an activist with a track record, a wind-down vote, a collapse of dual-class
shares, a tender at NAV.
Key documents: the holdco's own annual report (look for related-party leakage and holdco-level
debt), the stake's filings, and any activist 13D or equivalent.

### 4 — Rights issues, recapitalisations and index events
Equity raises where the structure itself creates the mispricing: deeply discounted rights
issues with overhang, nil-paid rights trading below theoretical value, rump placements, and
forced flow around index deletions and additions.
Look at: rights issues by sound businesses where the discount and the overhang depress the
stock for weeks; index deletions of names that no longer belong in the deleted index but are
not yet in the next one; large secondary block sales by exiting sponsors.
This lens has the shortest shelf life and also feeds the fast sleeve. Act within weeks or
skip it. Never buy the rights themselves; buy the equity, or buy the equity ex-rights after
the rump.
Key documents: the rights issue prospectus, the index provider's rebalance announcement,
the sponsor's sale prospectus.

### 5 — Regulated assets and Brazilian special situations
Two pockets where the investor has a genuine information edge from the day job, deliberately
constrained by the mandate.
**Regulated assets:** listed water, gas, power and transport concessions facing a tariff
review, a concession renewal, a privatisation, or a regulatory decision with a date.
Argentine and Brazilian regulated utilities count, and so do European and Latin American ones
where the framework is readable. The mispricing is usually the market pricing the last
regulatory regime rather than the announced one.
**Brazilian special situations:** recuperação judicial exits, B3 tender offers (OPAs) and
delistings, corporate reorganisations, and companies where a Lei 11.101 process has completed
and the equity is ignored. **Everything here sits inside the 0 to 10% Brazil cap** and is
subject to the leverage and financial-sector exclusions. The memo also confirms, every time,
that the name is not something Starboard is active in or restricted on; if the answer is
unclear, the idea goes to the watchlist as a paper position and is not proposed.
Key documents: the regulator's decision or consultation, the concession contract, the plano
de recuperação judicial, the edital de OPA.

### 6 — Portfolio week. No new ideas.
No screening. No candidates. The entire memo is: a full re-underwrite of every existing
position, a check of every kill criterion and every catalyst date for whether it is still the
right one, a rebalance check against bands, and a review of what the last eight weeks of memos
got wrong. Paper positions in the watchlist get the same treatment; that record is the point of
phase 1.
**Resist the urge to add an idea "since we're here."**

### 7 — Anti-thesis week
Take the three largest satellite positions, or the three strongest watchlist theses if the
satellite is not yet open, and argue actively for selling or dropping each. Build the
strongest bear case using sources that disagree: short reports, competitor filings, the other
side of the transaction, regulatory objections, delay scenarios. For event-driven positions
the bear case is specifically: what if the event does not happen, or happens two quarters
late? The output is a verdict on each: hold, trim, exit, or drop from the watchlist.
This is the most valuable week of the cycle and the one most likely to be skipped. Do not skip it.

## Phase 1 note (portfolio starts empty)

As of 2026-09-09 the IBKR account holds **no positions**. Per the mandate, the satellite and
fast sleeve open only at phase 2 (around US$12,500 invested). Until then every candidate from
lenses 0 to 5 is a **paper position**: logged with entry price and date, thesis, catalyst
date, target, kill criteria and a hypothetical size that would clear the hard limits. Scored
at each gate as if it were real, after commissions, spreads, FX and the 15% tax.

The paper record is not a rehearsal. It is the track record the Starboard proposal will be
built on, so it is kept to the same standard as a live book: nothing added retroactively,
nothing dropped quietly, rejected ideas logged with the reason.

## Where to actually look

Primary sources, in rough order of usefulness for this mandate:

- SEC EDGAR: Form 10-12B, 8-K (emergence, merger agreements), DEFM14A and SC TO-T (proxies and
  tenders), 13D (activists), S-1 and 424B (rights and rump prospectuses)
- Bankruptcy dockets and claims agents (Kroll, Epiq, Stretto) for plans and disclosure
  statements; the UK Insolvency Service and Companies House for schemes and administrations
- Exchange regulatory feeds: LSE RNS, SEDAR+, TDnet, Nasdaq Nordic, Euronext
- CVM: fatos relevantes, editais de OPA, planos de recuperação judicial via the court and the
  administrador judicial's site; ANBIMA for debenture terms
- Regulators: ENRE and ENARGAS (Argentina), ARSESP, ANEEL and ANTT (Brazil), Ofwat and Ofgem
  (UK), and the concession contracts themselves
- Index providers' rebalance announcements (MSCI, FTSE, S&P, STOXX, B3)
- Competitor and counterparty filings, which are often the most honest source on a situation

Treat aggregators, screeners, event calendars and financial media as pointers to check, never
as the source of a number or a date that goes in the memo.
